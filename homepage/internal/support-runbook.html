v0.2 · v1-launch scope
Right-sized for current support headcount: two tiers (Standard + Premium), business-hours coverage, no 24/7 commitment. A future Enterprise tier (24/7, 1-hr SLA, proactive outreach) is sketched in §07 / §10 but not promised to customers yet. Loosening a published SLA is brand damage; tightening it later is a press release. We start conservative.
01

North star

Acknowledge fast, fix correctly, never disappear. With limited tech resources, the customer-facing differentiator we can sustainably win on is responsiveness, not headcount. A 4-hour acknowledge on a critical ticket feels world-class to a Salesforce admin who's used to 2-day responses from larger ISVs.

Acknowledgement is the metric we audit. Fix-time depends on the issue and is not a public SLA.

Honesty check · "Named CSM"
Premium at 25% of a $6k/mo license = ~$18k/yr per customer. That funds fractional CSM time, not a full headcount. The customer-visible promise is one person owns the relationship — same email, same Slack handle, same QBR host — which is achievable. Do not let any salesperson use the phrase "dedicated CSM" in writing. One CSM can own ~15–25 Premium accounts at this scale.
What we are not promising at v1
  • 24/7 critical support — no on-call rotation; no overnight pages
  • 1-hour SLA — we publish 4-hour, business hours
  • Proactive outreach on production exceptions — we receive the alerts (§07) but do not commit to a customer-facing action yet
  • "Dedicated" anything — Premium gets named contacts, not exclusive headcount
These are reserved for an Enterprise tier launched once we have ≥5 customers asking for them and the headcount to staff them.
03

Intake · one channel, no exceptions

All support requests land in one ticketing system. Slack DMs, engineer email, and CSM email are not support channels. This is the single rule that keeps SLAs honest.

  • Customer email → support@datasyncpro.io → auto-creates ticket
  • In-product → "Get help" surface (post-launch) → auto-creates ticket with org context
  • CSM-relayed → CSM creates ticket on customer's behalf, never resolves out-of-system

Every ticket carries the customer's tier as a tag on creation (tier:standard / tier:premium), sourced from CRM via org-id lookup. The router cannot make tier-aware decisions without this tag, so it is mandatory.

04

Severity matrix · published to customers

"Critical" cannot be subjective. We define it once, publish it on the website, and reject mis-tagged tickets back to the customer with a brief explanation. Customer-facing labels are Critical / High / Normal; we use Sev 1 / 2 / 3 internally.

Severity Definition Examples
Sev 1
CRITICAL		 Production Executable broken or risk of data corruption. No workaround. Business impact ongoing. Batch failing on every run · Trigger writing wrong values · Data Loader corrupting records · Cross-org sync silently dropping rows
Sev 2
HIGH		 Functional but degraded. Workaround exists. Affects production but not blocking. Action Button intermittent · Query Manager slow on one object · Scheduled job late but not failing
Sev 3
NORMAL		 Question, configuration help, low-impact bug, feature request. "How do I…" · cosmetic issues · sandbox-only · enhancement requests
05

Acknowledge SLAs · how we measure them

"Acknowledge" = a human has read the ticket, set severity, and posted an initial response to the customer. Auto-replies do not count. SLA clock runs from ticket.created_at to first_human_response_at.

TierSev 1 · CriticalSev 2 · HighSev 3 · Normal
Standard2 business days2 business days2 business days
Premium4 hours (extended hrs)1 business day2 business days

Business hours = 9am–5pm customer time zone, Mon–Fri. Extended hours for Premium critical tickets = 7am–9pm customer time, Mon–Fri. No coverage outside that window at v1.

Audit cadence
Ops pulls the SLA-attainment report on the first Monday of every month. Any miss in the prior month is logged with a root cause: under-staffed shift, mis-tagged tier, delayed triage, or misclassified severity. Attainment is shared with each Premium customer in their semi-annual review. We do not hide misses.
06

Triage rotation · business-hours model

With no 24/7 commitment, the rotation is extended-hours, not always-on. Minimum viable team: 2 engineers (primary + backup). No PagerDuty pager, no overnight wakeups, no on-call stipend. We can scale to 3 + add a real on-call rotation when (and only when) Enterprise tier launches.

Primary triage (weekly)

  • Owns the support inbox during their shift week
  • Acknowledges new tickets per SLA
  • Assigns severity; triages or hands off to subject expert
  • Extended-hours window for Premium criticals: 7am–9pm Mon–Fri

Backup

  • Covers when primary is in meetings, sick, or on PTO
  • Auto-paged if a Premium Sev 1 sits unacknowledged for 1 hour
  • Same shift length as primary
ToolTicketing system's native notification + Slack channel #support-triage. No PagerDuty yet (we don't have an SLA that requires it).
Shift length1 week. Handoff Monday 10am via written handoff doc covering open Sev 1/Sev 2 tickets.
After-hoursNone. Tickets created outside business hours are ack'd next business morning. This is consistent with the public SLA — do not improvise off-hours responses, even when tempted; it creates inconsistent expectations.
Escalation contactVP Engineering for any Sev 1 unack'd past 4-hour Premium SLA.
07

Exception alerts Future · Enterprise tier

Salesforce emails the package's contact address whenever an unhandled exception fires in any installed customer's org. We receive these today, for every tier. What we do with them at v1 vs. when Enterprise launches:

PhaseWhat happens with the alert
v1 (today) Archived to a searchable inbox. Indexed for product-feedback signal (which Executables fail most, which customer profiles trip them). No customer-facing action. No ticket is created on the customer's behalf. We do not pre-emptively contact.
Future · Enterprise Route to a tier-aware ticketing flow. Auto-file ticket on customer's queue for matching alert patterns. CSM is notified and contacts the customer within 1 business day with a starting diagnosis. This is the headline Enterprise differentiator: "we reach out first."

Today's action item
Build the receive + archive path now (parse-and-index inbox), even though we don't act on it customer-facing. Two reasons:
  1. Product feedback signal. Tells us which Executables fail most often and where to invest engineering time.
  2. Pre-staging for Enterprise. When Enterprise launches, we already have months of training data for the suppression rules — instead of being firehosed on day 1.

09

Open gaps before v1 is publish-ready

  1. Ticketing system selection. Recommend Linear (eng-friendly) or HubSpot Service Hub (CRM-integrated). Decide by EOW.
  2. Org-id → tier lookup service. Lightweight at v1 (2 tiers). Eng work: ~1 day.
  3. Severity-matrix public page. Add to /support or as a section on Pricing. Customer-facing labels: Critical / High / Normal.
  4. Extended-hours staffing decision. Who covers 7am–9pm Mon–Fri? Either expand 2 engineers' hours or hire one offshore (later).
  5. Exception alert inbox (archive-only). Eng work: ~2 days. Sets us up for Enterprise without committing to anything customer-facing today.
  6. CSM onboarding playbook. What does the named-CSM relationship look like? Cadence, channels, deliverables. One Google Doc.
10

Roadmap · what unlocks the Enterprise tier

We launch a third tier (Enterprise) when all four of these conditions are true. Not before. Each one is a real cost; bundling them lets us name a price that funds them honestly.

ConditionWhat "ready" means
Customer demand≥5 Premium customers explicitly asking for 24/7 or proactive outreach in QBRs
Eng headcount≥3 support-capable engineers for on-call rotation (no burnout math)
Alert routingReceive + parse + archive path running in production for ≥3 months, suppression rules tuned
CSM playbookTriage criteria + outreach template + cadence documented and rehearsed

Enterprise tier likely lands at ~35% of license, bundling: 24/7 critical · 1-hr Sev 1 SLA · 8 hr/mo solution architect · dedicated CSM · proactive exception outreach.
